Tulasi (Holy Basil) Plant Information

Scientific Name: Ocimum tenuiflorum (also known as Ocimum sanctum)

Common Names: Tulsi, Holy Basil

Family: Lamiaceae (Mint family)

Native To: Indian subcontinent and Southeast Asia

Most Found In: India, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Thailand, and some parts of Africa

Water Requirements

Moderate watering is required.

Water 2–3 times per week in summer and once a week in winter, depending on soil moisture.

Overwatering can cause root rot, while underwatering can slow growth.

Uses of Tulasi

1. Medicinal Uses:

Used in Ayurveda to treat colds, coughs, fevers, and respiratory issues.

Acts as an immunity booster and has antibacterial, antiviral, and antifungal properties.

Helps in reducing stress and anxiety due to its adaptogenic properties.

2. Religious & Spiritual Significance:

Sacred in Hinduism; often planted in homes and temples.

Used in religious rituals and prayers.

3. Culinary Uses:

Leaves are used in herbal teas, soups, and seasoning.

Tulsi tea is popular for its health benefits.

4. Environmental Benefits:

Acts as a natural air purifier by releasing oxygen.

Repels mosquitoes and other insects.

5. Skincare & Beauty:

Used in herbal face packs for acne and skin infections.

Tulsi oil is used in hair and skincare products.

Growth Conditions

Soil: Well-draining, loamy or sandy soil.

Sunlight: Requires 4–6 hours of direct sunlight daily.

Temperature: Thrives in warm climates (15–35°C).
